Man launched 92ft in the air in record-breaking water airbag catapult stunt

byMatthew Benson
Published: Aug 30, 2026, 15:37
HLFotoFilm / YouTube
Add as a preferred source on GoogleFollow on Google News

A man was launched to a record-breaking height of 92ft in the air after three other men jumped on a water airbag.

Adrian and Eric Baumann, owners of experience provider Paintballfarm, have been organizing “blobbing” events for years. “Blobbing” is an activity that sees someone sat at the end of a large, partially inflated airbag (the blob), and being launched into the air.

The pair teamed up with a four-person unit comprised of Robin Steiner and Luc Siegenthaler from Switzerland, Lennart Florczak from Germany, and Tristan Kuhn from the USA, to attempt the world record for biggest height achieved from blobbing.

Article continues after ad

The previous record was 72ft in 2012

A team of Germans achieved a height of 72 ft (22m) in 2012, and this record stood until 2026 when the Baumanns and their four-person team came along and attempted to break it. On their first attempt, they broke the record with a height of 75ft (23m), but wanted to push it further.

Article continues after ad

On their second attempt, after a brief personnel change following a small injury, Steiner was the individual positioned at the end of the airbag while Siegenthaler, Florczak, and Kuhn were the trio jumping onto it.

Article continues after ad

According to the Guinness World Records, Steiner reached a height of 92.58ft (28.22m), securing the record for greatest height achieved launched from an airbag (blobbing).

Steiner described the feeling as “a car crash in reverse”, but one of the most incredible experiences of his life. He also said that the force of being launched was much more intense than the crash into the water.

Adrian Baumann spoke highly of the whole experience: “It was simply incredible. Everyone was absolutely thrilled, and the spectators went completely crazy.”

Couple discover mysterious 150-pound sculpture buried in their backyard

byMatthew Benson
Published: Aug 30, 2026, 12:20
Add as a preferred source on GoogleFollow on Google News

A couple found a mysterious 150-pound limestone sculpture buried in their backyard, prompting a search to uncover why it was there.

Article continues after ad

Greg Gatwood was doing work in the backyard of the San Francisco home he shares with his partner Judy Gittelsohn, when he hit what he believed to be a piece of concrete buried in the dirt.

Gatwood used a shovel to try and free the object, before eventually using a pickaxe. It was only when he saw lips and a nose looking back at him that he realized it was really a statue.

Article continues after ad

It was 150lbs and nearly two feet wide

Gatwood told Fox News that the statue was “like something you see in a movie, almost cinematic” when he pulled it out of the ground and showed it to Gittelsohn. His partner said she was “gobsmacked” by the “enormous find”.

Gittelsohn, as a professional artist herself, researched the sculpture. She believes it dates back to the 1930s, and may be connected to the Works Progress Administration (WPA), an American New Deal agency created by Franklin D. Roosevelt to provide income to the unemployed during the depression.

Their home is located near the former San Francisco Art Institute, at which famous sculptor Ralph Stackpole taught for a number of decades. Because the sculpture is also made from inexpensive limestone, Gittelsohn believes it may be connected to one of Stackpole’s students.

The sculpture received much online attention, including one woman who appeared at their door and told them she had previously held an exhibition featuring the Stackpole’s work. In September, the couple will put the sculpture on display at the American Messenger Gallery in the hope that someone recognises it.
