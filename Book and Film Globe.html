Fifteen Books For a Spectacular September

From Emily St. John Mandel to Chloe Benjamin; Ayad Akhtar to Marlon Benjamin; Tom Clancy to Jared Diamond

September is traditionally when publishers begin rolling out their biggest books of the year, and 2026 is no exception. This month’s list brings together long-awaited novels from some of the world’s finest literary writers, ambitious works of speculative fiction, gripping commercial thrillers, and thoughtful nonfiction tackling everything from artificial intelligence to ancient monarchies. Whether you’re looking for the season’s likely prize contenders or simply your next unforgettable read, these are the books we’re most excited to see arriving this September.

    • Exit Party — Emily St. John Mandel
      Emily St. John Mandel has become one of the few contemporary novelists equally beloved by literary readers and science-fiction fans. Following Station Eleven, The Glass Hotel, and Sea of Tranquility, she returns with another mind-bending novel in which a single party in a fractured near-future Los Angeles sends ripples across parallel lives and alternate realities. Elegant, suspenseful, and intellectually adventurous, Exit Party looks set to be one of the defining novels of the autumn.
      Publisher: Knopf; Release date: September 15, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)
        • Under Story — Chloe Benjamin
          Since The Immortalists, Chloe Benjamin has shown a rare ability to blend intimate family drama with boldly imaginative ideas. Her latest novel begins at an Antarctic research station, where a disgraced biologist encounters a mysterious phenomenon that draws her back toward the family and scientific career she abandoned. Part love story, part speculative epic, and part meditation on grief, Under Story asks how far we would go for more time with the people we love.
          Publisher: G.P. Putnam’s Sons; Release date: September 1, 2026
          Order/Preorder (Bookshop)
    • The Radiance — Ayad Akhtar
      Pulitzer Prize winner Ayad Akhtar follows Homeland Elegies with another intellectually fearless novel. Blending campus intrigue, philosophical inquiry, and spiritual mystery, The Radiance explores the uneasy territory between faith, belief, and obsession. Akhtar has become one of America’s most provocative literary voices.
      Publisher: Summit Books; Release date: September 29, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • The Disappearers — Marlon James
      This novel has already been longlisted for the Booker Prize! After detouring into epic fantasy with his acclaimed Dark Star Trilogy, Booker Prize winner Marlon James returns to contemporary Jamaica. Centered on the brutal assault of a group of gay actors in 1980s Kingston, The Disappearers examines violence, identity, memory, and survival with the fearless ambition that has made James one of the most important novelists writing in English today.
      Publisher: Riverhead Books; Release date: September 1, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • The Rouse — China Miéville
      China Miéville has spent nearly twenty years writing what may prove to be his masterpiece. Stretching across continents and decades, The Rouse combines political conspiracy, supernatural mystery, and personal grief in the kind of intellectually adventurous epic that only Miéville could attempt. The author of Perdido Street Station and The City & the City has long been one of speculative fiction’s most original voices, and early reports suggest this long-awaited novel more than justifies the anticipation.
      Publisher: Del Rey; Release date: September 15, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • Scion — James Islington
      James Islington has become one of fantasy’s fastest-rising stars thanks to The Licanius Trilogy and The Will of the Many. With Scion, he launches a brand-new standalone adventure, promising the intricate plotting, moral complexity, and exhilarating world-building that have earned him a devoted international following.
      Publisher: Saga Press; Release date: September 1, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • The Passing of the Dragon and Other Stories — Ken Liu
      Few contemporary writers move as effortlessly between science fiction, fantasy, history, and literary fiction as Ken Liu. Best known for The Paper Menagerie and his translations of Cixin Liu’s The Three-Body Problem, Liu returns with a new collection that ranges from intimate character studies to sweeping speculative epics. Like his finest work, these stories use fantastical ideas not as ends in themselves but as ways of asking timeless questions about memory, technology, identity, and what it means to be human.
      Publisher: Saga Press; Release date: September 8, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • Mother Daughter Sister Stranger — Hank Phillippi Ryan
      Award-winning crime writer Hank Phillippi Ryan has built a reputation for intricate psychological suspense rooted in believable family relationships. Her latest novel begins with an unexpected reunion but quickly spirals into deception, long-buried secrets, and impossible choices. Ryan has an instinctive understanding of how ordinary lives can unravel under extraordinary pressure, making this another strong recommendation for readers who like their thrillers driven as much by character as by plot.
      Publisher: Minotaur Books; Release date: September 1, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • Tom Clancy: Pressure Depth — Jack Stewart
      The Tom Clancy franchise continues with a new globe-trotting thriller featuring the geopolitical intrigue, military technology, and high-stakes action that have defined the series for more than four decades. Jack Stewart brings a contemporary sensibility to Clancy’s universe while retaining the meticulous attention to operational detail that longtime fans expect. If you’re looking for an old-fashioned international page-turner to balance September’s heavier literary offerings, this fits the bill.Publisher: G.P. Putnam’s Sons;
      Release date: September 1, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

      • Profits, Prophets, Coaches, and Kings — Jared Diamond
        Pulitzer Prize winner Jared Diamond has spent decades explaining why civilizations succeed, fail, and evolve. In his latest book he turns his attention to leadership itself, asking why some individuals inspire flourishing societies while others leave institutions weaker than they found them. Drawing on history, anthropology, psychology, and contemporary politics, Diamond once again tackles enormous questions with the clarity that made Guns, Germs, and Steel and Collapse international bestsellers.
        Publisher: Mariner; Release date: September 1, 2026
        Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

      • Queens and Kings — Lucy Worsley
        Television historian and bestselling author Lucy Worsley has an unmatched gift for making the past feel immediate. In Queens and Kings she re-examines the British monarchy through the relationships, rivalries, and personalities that shaped the crown over centuries. Combining meticulous scholarship with an engaging narrative voice, Worsley continues to demonstrate why she has become one of today’s most popular public historians.
        Publisher: Pegasus Books; Release date: September 1, 2026
        Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

      • This Is About Running. This Is Not About Running. — Chris Bennett
        Running memoirs have become a genre unto themselves, but Chris Bennett’s debut promises something more expansive than tales of finish lines and personal bests. Using endurance sport as a starting point, Bennett reflects on grief, resilience, friendship, and the stories we tell ourselves to keep moving forward. Whether you’re an experienced marathoner or someone who has never laced up a pair of running shoes, this is a book about the meaning we find in perseverance rather than athletic achievement.
        Publisher: Dey Street; Release date: September 1, 2026
        Order/Preorder (Bookshop)
    • American Hagwon — Min Jin Lee
      Nearly a decade after Pachinko became an international phenomenon, Min Jin Lee returns with another sweeping novel of the Korean diaspora. Beginning in the aftermath of the Asian financial crisis and stretching from South Korea to Australia and Southern California, American Hagwon follows one family’s struggle to redefine success in a world where education has become both aspiration and obsession.
      Publisher: Cardinal; Release date: September 29, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • The American Way of Killing: The Invention of an Epidemic — Malcolm Gladwell
      Malcolm Gladwell has spent a quarter century persuading readers to see familiar problems from unexpected angles. His latest book turns that trademark curiosity toward America’s uniquely persistent epidemic of gun violence. Combining archival research, original reporting, social science, and Gladwell’s gift for surprising connections, The American Way of Killing argues that the roots of the crisis lie in places most conventional debates never examine.
      Publisher: Little, Brown and Company; Release date: September 29, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)

    • Personal Responsibility, Inc — Julie Tilsen
      At a moment when neoliberalism is “natural” and therapy has become a default response to everything from workplace burnout to political despair, psychotherapist Julie Tilsen asks whether many of our “individual” problems are actually systemic? In her new book, the author of Queering Your Therapy Practice critiques society in a call to rethink what healing might really require.
      Publisher: OR Books; Release date: September 15, 2026
      Order/Preorder (Bookshop)
