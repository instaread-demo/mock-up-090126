Millbrook veteran's PTSD triggered by noisy neighbour

31st August
By Xavi Straw Reporter

A Millbrook veteran has alleged his PTSD his worsening thanks to verbal abuse from a nightmare neighbour.

Lee Greenwood, 65, a decorated Gulf War veteran, moved into his Toronto Court flat after seven years of homelessness.

Since then he has struggled with a noisy neighbour who regularly bangs on the floor with what he suspects to be a hammer or walking stick.

He said: “It brings back memories of the war once she starts banging and slamming, and after all these years it’s getting to me. I’m now self-harming on a regular basis.

“I love it here; I’ve got good friends and good neighbours; it’s just her that’s pushed me to the point where I don’t even want to be here.

“Sometimes I think I’d be better off back on the streets, it’s that bad.”

Lee Greenwood's medals from 11 years of military service. (Image: Xavi Straw)

The residents said their complaints have led the council to moves her three times in the past four years.

Terry Sinnett, 72, Lee’s friend and fellow neighbour, said he was the best neighbour she had ever had after saving her late husband’s life following a fall.

She said: “Lee’s the one that suffers most.

“Every time she’s banging it’s bringing back Lee’s PTSD, so he’s really suffering with it.

“We don’t want to, but all of us have thought once or twice about moving.”

Katerina Wheeldon, 62, said the neighbour verbally abused her when they first met more than two years ago and has regularly woken her between 2am and 4am ever since.

She said: “She’s kept me and my husband awake several times, it’s honestly horrible.

“It’s getting too much, we’re absolutely sick of it. Something’s got to be done, she shouldn’t be allowed to wreck people’s lives like this.

“She’s made everybody’s life a misery around here.”

Councillor Sally Goodfellow said: "We take all reports of anti-social behaviour extremely seriously and understand the impact this can have on residents. 

"We are working closely with tenants at Toronto Court in response to concerns that have been raised along with relevant partner agencies to ensure that residents are supported and appropriate action is taken where necessary."

The neighbour was approached by the Echo for comment but declined to respond.
